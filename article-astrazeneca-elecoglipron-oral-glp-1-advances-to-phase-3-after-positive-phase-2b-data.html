AstraZeneca's Oral GLP-1 Pill Elecoglipron Advances to Phase 3 After Strong Phase 2b Results

By Mike Allen•PeptideAAA•September 25, 2026

AstraZeneca is moving its oral GLP-1 receptor agonist elecoglipron into Phase 3 development, betting that a once-daily pill without the complex food-and-water restrictions of some earlier oral GLP-1 attempts can carve out space in a market currently dominated by injectable drugs.

What Elecoglipron Is

Elecoglipron is a small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist taken as a once-daily oral tablet. Unlike peptide-based GLP-1 drugs, which are typically injected because they break down in the digestive tract, elecoglipron is a non-peptide small molecule designed specifically to survive oral dosing. It's being developed for three separate indications, type 2 diabetes, obesity and overweight, and chronic kidney disease, with cardiovascular outcome trials also planned.

The Phase 2b Data Behind the Decision

AstraZeneca's Phase 3 decision follows two separate Phase 2b studies. In the VISTA obesity trial, participants taking elecoglipron lost an average of 10.5% of body weight at 26 weeks, compared with 0.6% on placebo, with weight loss continuing to 11.8% by week 36; nearly 89% of participants achieved at least 5% weight loss. In the SOLSTICE diabetes trial, participants' average HbA1c, a measure of blood sugar control over roughly three months, fell by 1.9 percentage points versus 0.2 points on placebo, with 90% of participants reaching an HbA1c below 7% and average body weight dropping 7.7% compared with 1.7% on placebo.

Why an Oral Pill Matters

Every GLP-1 drug currently approved for weight loss in the U.S. is either an injection or, in the case of oral semaglutide, a pill with meaningful dietary restrictions around timing, food, and water intake. A once-daily pill without those restrictions, if it holds up through Phase 3 and regulatory review, could lower a real barrier to starting and staying on GLP-1 therapy for patients who are needle-averse or for whom the logistics of injectable drugs are a genuine obstacle.

Regulatory Status

Elecoglipron is not FDA-approved and remains an investigational drug. Phase 2b results are not a substitute for the larger, longer Phase 3 trials AstraZeneca now needs to run, and strong performance in a few hundred participants over 36 weeks doesn't guarantee similar results in the thousands of patients, more diverse populations, and longer timeframes that Phase 3 and FDA review require.

Where This Fits in the Oral GLP-1 Race

AstraZeneca joins a growing field of companies chasing a "better" oral GLP-1 option. Eli Lilly's orforglipron became the first oral GLP-1 pill without food or water restrictions, and Novo Nordisk has brought a high-dose oral semaglutide tablet to market for weight management. Elecoglipron's Phase 2b data suggest AstraZeneca believes it can compete on both efficacy and convenience, though it is arriving to that race later than its rivals.

The Bottom Line

AstraZeneca's move to Phase 3 is a genuine vote of confidence in its own data, but it's still an early step. Elecoglipron should be read the way any Phase 2 GLP-1 candidate should: promising numbers, an unapproved drug, and years of trial work still ahead before it could reach patients.
